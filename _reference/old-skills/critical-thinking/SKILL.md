# Critical Thinking Deep Dive

This skill exists because quick frameworks catch the obvious stuff but miss the corners. When something matters enough to think about properly — a person, a strategy, a commitment, a decision that affects people — you need to look from every angle, not just the 3-4 that jump out first.

Based on "The Ultimate Cheatsheet for Critical Thinking" (saved: `data/source-docs/critical-thinking-questions-cheatsheet.png`) plus a Gordon-specific personal filter developed from T118.

---

## When This Triggers

- Gordon says "critical thinking," "deep dive," "think deeper," "let's really look at this," "I want to think about this properly"
- Think-first's critical thinking deep dive tickler fires and Gordon accepts
- Gordon is evaluating a person, a relationship, a high-stakes business decision, a strategic commitment, or anything involving trust
- Gordon explicitly invokes `/critical-thinking`

**Do NOT trigger on:**
- Quick decisions that think-first handles fine
- Mechanical tasks, status checks, file ops
- When Gordon says "just do it" or signals urgency

---

## How It Works

Walk all 7 dimensions. Every time. No skipping.

Within each dimension, surface the questions most relevant to the specific topic — don't robotically recite all 48+. But every dimension gets airtime. The power is in the coverage, not the individual questions.

**Posture:** Same as think-first analysis mode — sharpen Gordon's thinking, don't think for him. Present what each dimension surfaces, ask the hard questions, let him drive.

## The 7 Dimensions

### 1. WHO

The people dimension. Who's involved, who's affected, who has power, who's invisible.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| Who benefits from this? | Always — the most important question in the set |
| Who does this harm? | Always — the complement to "who benefits" |
| Who makes decisions about this? | Power structures, organizations, negotiations |
| Who is most directly affected? | Any change that touches people's lives |
| Who has discussed this? | Prior art, existing conversations, politics |
| Who would be best to consult? | Unfamiliar territory, expertise gaps |
| Who would be the key person in this? | Stakeholder identification, coalition building |
| Who deserves recognition for this? | Team dynamics, credit, relationship maintenance |

### 2. WHAT

The substance dimension. What's actually happening, what are the alternatives, what's strong and weak.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| What are the strengths or weaknesses? | Evaluating anything — a plan, a person, an idea |
| What is another perspective? | Any time you feel certain — that's when you need this most |
| What is another alternative? | Before committing to a path — generate options first |
| What would be a counter-argument? | Any proposal, especially ones that feel obviously right |
| What is the best or worst case scenario? | Bracketing outcomes before committing |
| What is most or least important? | Prioritization, scope, focus |
| What can we do to make a positive change? | Pivot from analysis to action |
| What is getting in the way of our action? | Identifying blockers, resistance, friction |

### 3. WHEN

The temporal dimension. Timing, history, urgency, patience.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| When is this acceptable/unacceptable? | Boundary setting, ethical lines, timing windows |
| When would this benefit society/others? | Broader impact, social good, legacy |
| When would this cause a problem? | Risk timing, seasonal patterns, market windows |
| When is the best time to take action? | Strategic timing — not just "now" vs "later" |
| When will we know we've succeeded? | Success criteria — define done BEFORE starting |
| When has this played a part in our history? | Historical precedent, pattern recognition |
| When can we expect this to change? | Patience calibration, realistic timelines |
| When should we ask for help? | Help-seeking triggers — don't go it alone too long |

### 4. WHERE

The spatial/contextual dimension. Where does this exist, where are the analogues, where to look.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| Where can we see this in the world? | Real-world examples, proof of concept |
| Where are similar concepts/situations? | Cross-domain pattern matching — the most underused thinking tool |
| Where is the most need for this? | Targeting, prioritization, market fit |
| Where would this be a problem? | Context-dependent risks, cultural/geographic factors |
| Where can we get more information? | Research sourcing, due diligence |
| Where do we get help with this? | Resource identification, partnerships |
| Where will this idea take us? | Trajectory thinking, long-term direction |
| Where are the areas for improvement? | Gap analysis, continuous improvement |

### 5. WHY

The motivation dimension. Purpose, relevance, inertia, accountability.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| Why is this a problem/challenge? | Root cause analysis — don't solve symptoms |
| Why is it relevant to me/others? | Personal relevance filter — does this actually matter? |
| Why is this the best/worst scenario? | Understanding the extremes, not just the middle |
| Why are people influenced by this? | Persuasion mechanics, social dynamics, incentives |
| Why should people know about this? | Communication need, awareness, transparency |
| Why has it been this way for so long? | Inertia analysis — the status quo persists for reasons |
| Why have we allowed this to happen? | Accountability, complicity, systems thinking |
| Why is there a need for this today? | Urgency assessment, timeliness, market readiness |

### 6. HOW

The mechanism dimension. Similarity, disruption, truth, safety, agency.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| How is this similar to ...? | Analogy finding — map to known patterns |
| How does this disrupt things? | Change impact, disruption assessment |
| How do we know the truth about this? | Source verification, information reliability, epistemics |
| How will we approach this safely? | Risk mitigation, reversibility, protecting downside |
| How does this benefit us or others? | Concrete benefit mapping |
| How does this harm us or others? | Concrete harm mapping — be specific |
| How do we see this in the future? | Future-casting, trajectory, long-term vision |
| How can we change this for our good? | Agency — moving from analysis to action |

### 7. SELF (The Gordon Layer)

The personal filter. Not on the original cheatsheet — built for Gordon specifically from T118. This dimension asks the questions that the analytical dimensions miss: the human ones.

| Question | When it matters most |
|----------|---------------------|
| How will this make people feel? | Any decision affecting relationships — the empathy check |
| Does this align with who I'm trying to be? | Values alignment — "Would future-me be proud of this?" |
| Do I have the energy/bandwidth for this right now? | Capacity check — ADHD/bipolar energy management |
| What's the kind way to communicate this sharp conclusion? | The dual-layer filter: sharp analysis inside, warm delivery outside |
| What would I tell a friend in this situation? | Perspective shift — advice vs. action gap |
| Am I reacting or responding? | Emotional regulation check — especially when something feels urgent |
| What am I afraid of here? | Fear audit — surface the thing that's driving avoidance or urgency |
| What would Leah say? | The relationship gut-check — her perspective often catches what Gordon's misses |

## Running the Deep Dive

### Step 1: Name the topic

State clearly what we're examining. One sentence.

*"The thing we're looking at is [X]."*

### Step 2: Walk the dimensions

Go through all 7. For each dimension:

1. **State the dimension** — "Let's look at WHO."
2. **Surface the 3-5 most relevant questions** from that dimension for this specific topic
3. **Present what those questions reveal** — observations, tensions, gaps, surprises
4. **Flag anything that needs Gordon's input** — don't guess on things that require his knowledge

Spend proportional time — some dimensions will be richer than others for any given topic. That's fine. But don't skip any.

### Step 3: Synthesis

After all 7 dimensions, pull it together:

- **Strongest signal:** What came through loudest across multiple dimensions?
- **Biggest gap:** What do we still not know that matters?
- **Hidden tension:** What contradicts what? (If a contradiction emerges, suggest `/triz`)
- **The question Gordon should sit with:** One hard question that emerged from the full scan

### Step 4: Let Gordon drive

Present the synthesis. Don't conclude. Don't recommend unless asked. Gordon steers from here.
