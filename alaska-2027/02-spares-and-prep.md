---

## The premise, stated plainly

> *"Is the idea that they have the skills but not the parts available?"*

**Yes. Exactly that.** A shop in Tok, Watson Lake, or Haines Junction can install a
crank position sensor in under an hour. What they cannot do is produce one. Parts for
a 5VZ-FE in a 1990 chassis come from Whitehorse, Fairbanks, or overnight from
Anchorage or Vancouver — and "overnight" up there means **2–7 days**, longer on a
weekend or a Canadian holiday.

The spares box is not a self-repair kit. **It is a wait-time reduction device.**
You hand the part to someone competent and drive away the same day instead of
sitting in Watson Lake until Thursday.

**Unlimited towing changes the math further.** A free tow to Whitehorse removes most
of the catastrophe risk. So the list should cover only the parts a real shop *wouldn't
have on the shelf* — not a full parts department.

⚠ One honest caveat on towing: unlimited coverage does not create tow trucks. There
are very few heavy-duty wreckers in the Yukon and northern BC, and the nearest one may
be 200 miles away and hours out. The coverage is excellent; the wait can still be long.

## A. Learn these five things — no mechanical skill required

Worth an hour in the driveway before departure. These are the failures that strand you
between towns, where waiting for a tow costs a day even when it's free.

1. **Plug a tire.** Kit + 12V compressor. Genuinely easy. Practice on a dead tire.
2. **Patch a split coolant hose** with self-fusing silicone tape. Gets you to town.
3. **Swap a fuse or relay.** Know where the boxes are and what's in them.
4. **Check and top fluids** — coolant, oil, ATF, brake, power steering.
5. **Read a code** with the OBD reader and know what it's telling you.

## B. Carry these — the part is the bottleneck, not the labor

### 5VZ-specific electrical (small box, ~0.5 cu ft, ~10 lbs) ★ highest value
A Whitehorse parts counter stocks for trucks on the road today, not a swapped
engine in a 1990 motorhome. **This box is the core of the whole list.**
- [ ] Ignition coil
- [ ] Distributor cap + rotor
- [ ] Spark plug wire set
- [ ] Spark plugs ×6
- [ ] Crankshaft position sensor
- [ ] Camshaft position sensor
- [ ] IAC valve
- [ ] Relay set (fuel pump, EFI, main, starter) + full fuse assortment

### Cooling (~0.6 cu ft, ~12 lbs)
Strands you between towns, and the 3" exhaust means more underhood heat than stock.
- [ ] Upper + lower radiator hoses
- [ ] Bulk heater hose + assorted clamps
- [ ] Thermostat + gasket, radiator cap
- [ ] **Self-fusing silicone repair tape** (the highest value item per ounce)
- [ ] 1 gal premix coolant
- [ ] Accessory belt set

### Alternator (~0.4 cu ft, ~12 lbs)
Trip-ender, no roadside workaround, and it's charging a large Victron bank —
more critical here than on a stock truck. Keep the rebuilt spare.

### Fluids for topping off, not for servicing (~0.4 cu ft, ~20 lbs)
- [ ] 1 gal engine oil, 1 oil filter
- [ ] 1 qt ATF, small brake fluid, small power steering fluid

### Tire + recovery (~1.0 cu ft, ~35 lbs)
- [ ] **Full-size spare** (mounted separately — dually, any position)
- [ ] Plug kit, 12V compressor, accurate gauge
- [ ] Bottle jack rated well above 7,500 lbs + base plate for soft ground
- [ ] Lug wrench that fits + breaker bar + torque wrench
- [ ] Tow strap + 2 D-rings

### Basic tools (~0.8 cu ft, ~30 lbs)
Metric sockets 10/12/14/17/19/22mm · 3/8" and 1/2" drive · breaker bar ·
combination wrenches · screwdrivers · needle-nose and channel-locks · vise grips ·
headlamp + work light · zip ties · duct tape · gloves · shop towels · multimeter

### Documents
- [ ] **OBD reader matched to the post-swap ECU generation** — verify which
- [ ] Factory service manual PDF on a tablet
- [ ] **ToyOnlySwaps build sheet / invoice** — so any shop knows what's actually in there
- [ ] Multiple spare keys, one hidden externally

## ⚠ Storage footprint

> *"Hitch mounted storage kills on length. How much space will that parts list take up?"*

| Version | Volume | Weight | Fits in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recommended (list above)** | **~3.7 cu ft** | **~120 lbs** | **One 27-gal tote + a small toolbox** |
| Full belt-and-suspenders list | ~7.5 cu ft | ~185 lbs | Two 27-gal totes + toolbox |

*Excludes the mounted spare tire.*

**Conclusion: the spares are not your storage problem.** One tote in an exterior bay
or under a dinette covers it. **Skip the hitch box** — you're at 21'2" against a
21'0" Going-to-the-Sun limit, and a receiver box makes that worse for no reason.

Note the 120 lbs against cargo capacity. **Weigh the rig loaded at a CAT scale**
before departure — axle-by-axle, not just gross.

## D. Pre-trip service — where the real money goes

### ⚠ The 5VZ situation — open questions
The engine was swapped into the **1988 Seabreeze** by **ToyOnlySwaps (Eugene, OR)**
a couple of years ago, with every offered upgrade. That is about the best provenance
available and removes most of my earlier concern.

Still need to establish:
- [ ] **Get the ToyOnlySwaps build sheet.** It will list exactly what was replaced —
      knock sensors, timing belt, water pump, seals. That determines what's left to do.
- [ ] **Miles on the timing belt since the shop did it.** 90k interval. A professional
      build doesn't exempt the belt from its service life.
- [ ] **Who is doing the transplant into the Sunrader?** ToyOnlySwaps again, or a
      different shop? A second removal-and-install is fresh opportunity for error,
      and the Sunrader-specific work — mounts, wiring, cooling routing, driveshaft
      length, exhaust routing — is all new regardless of how good the engine is.
- [ ] Cooling capacity for the Sunrader: radiator, fan clutch, and shroud sized for
      7,500 lbs on grades, not for the Seabreeze's weight.

### Chassis — 37 years old regardless of the frame-off
- Ball joints · tie rod ends · idler arm · steering box play · drag link
- Driveshaft U-joints + carrier bearing
- Wheel bearings repacked front and rear
- Brakes: pads, shoes, wheel cylinders, calipers, **all flexible lines**
- Full fluid change: engine, transmission, differential, brakes, power steering

### House side
- Roof seams and every penetration resealed (fiberglass shell — chronic Sunrader issue)
- Propane leak test + regulator (low use now — DC fridge — but still test)
- All lights and wiring
- Wheel torque spec written down and taped inside a cabinet

### ⚠ SOS — still open
Starlink on 12V DC and not permanently mounted is more robust than I assumed —
fair correction. The remaining gap isn't hardware reliability, it's that Starlink
requires *you* to be conscious and able to set it up.

**Recommendation: Garmin inReach Messenger or Mini 2** over an EPIRB/PLB.
| | inReach | PLB (ACR ResQLink) |
|---|---|---|
| SOS | ✓ via Garmin Response | ✓ direct to COSPAS-SARSAT |
| **Two-way messaging** | **✓** | ✗ |
| Tracking others can follow | ✓ | ✗ |
| Subscription | ~$15–50/mo, pausable | none |

The two-way messaging is why. Solo, most bad days aren't emergencies — they're
"broken down 40 miles past Iskut, not hurt, here's my position." A PLB can only
scream for helicopters. Note an EPIRB is specifically the maritime device; the land
equivalent is a PLB.
