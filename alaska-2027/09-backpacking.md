# Backpacking

Gordon has backpacking gear and multinight experience. **Solo.**

This is a strong addition: it converts surplus days into deep engagement, costs almost
nothing, and directly answers the boredom problem — the difference between looking at
a wilderness and being in it.

---

## ★ The two that need no permit and no rerouting

### 1. Kesugi Ridge — Denali State Park
**~27 mi, 3 days. No permit required.**
Above treeline the whole way along a tundra ridge with **the best view of Denali
available on foot** — better than anything inside the national park, which mostly
can't see the mountain. Trailheads (Little Coal Creek, Ermine Hill, Byers Lake) are
directly on the Parks Highway **between Talkeetna and Denali NP**. Slots into the
existing route with zero disruption.

### 2. Root Glacier & Donoho Basin — Wrangell–St. Elias
**2–3 days. No permit, no quota, no fees.** Largest national park in the US and
almost nobody in it. Walk out of Kennecott onto the glacier, cross to Donoho Basin,
camp among icebergs.
⚠ **Requires microspikes or crampons** and basic glacier judgment. Guided options
exist out of McCarthy if the crevasse assessment feels above your pay grade solo.

## ⚠ Chilkoot Trail — verify before planning around it
33 mi, Skagway over the pass to Bennett Lake, following the actual 1898 stampeder
route with artifacts still lying on the ground. Return by White Pass & Yukon Route
train. It is the best historic backpack on the continent and **Skagway is already in
the route (~Jul 12–14).**

**But: as of the 2026 season the international border at Chilkoot Pass was closed to
through travel** — out-and-back only on each side, no full traverse.
- [ ] ⚠ **Check 2027 status.** If the border reopens, this becomes a headline item.
- Out-and-back on the US side to Sheep Camp (~13 mi) or up to the pass (~16.5 mi)
  is still excellent and available either way.
- Permits: **Parks Canada reservations launch ~early February** (2026 launched Feb 5,
  9am MST); **US side opened April 15**, via Recreation.gov or 1-877-444-6777.
- Mandatory **in-person safety orientation** at the Skagway Trail Center to validate.

### ⚠ On Denali backcountry specifically
Free permits, unit quotas, bear-resistant food containers loaned at no charge. But
there are **no trails** — it is off-trail wilderness travel with unbridged glacial
river crossings, and with the road closed at Mile 43 the accessible units are limited.
**This is the most serious solo option on the list.** Kesugi Ridge delivers better
views for a fraction of the risk.

## Solo considerations
- **The inReach becomes load-bearing, not optional.** Leave an itinerary with someone
  and carry the device on your body, not in the pack lid.
- **Bear spray — buy in country**, you can't fly with it. Carry it accessible.
- Bear canister: required in Denali, worth it everywhere. Loaned free at Denali.
- Solo in grizzly country: make noise, no headphones, cook and store well away from
  the tent.
- Prefer **marked trails with other people on them** (Chilkoot, Kesugi) over off-trail
  solitude (Denali backcountry) for the serious mileage.

# Session 4 update — Denali yes, glacier no

**Root Glacier / Donoho Basin dropped.** No glacier travel wanted.
**Denali backcountry is in.** Here's what it actually involves.

## Denali NP backcountry — how it works
- **Permits are free but in-person only**, day-before or day-of, at the Backcountry
  Information Center. No advance reservations. Bring flexibility.
- The park is divided into **units, each with a nightly quota.** You pick units, not
  campsites. Max 7 nights in one unit.
- **Bear-resistant food containers are required and loaned free.**
- Mandatory backcountry orientation and a safety video before the permit issues.
- ⚠ **With the road closed at Mile 43, the reachable units are limited** to those served
  by the open section plus what the shuttle can reach. Check which units are actually
  in play for 2027 — this materially changes the options.

## What it's actually like
**There are no trails.** You navigate off-trail across tundra, brush, and gravel bars,
and you ford unbridged glacial rivers that run high and cold on warm afternoons. Tussock
tundra is genuinely exhausting — a mile an hour is a normal pace. This is the real thing,
not a signed route.

Solo, the things that matter most: conservative river-crossing decisions (turn around
rather than commit), the inReach on your body, and picking units with gravel-bar travel
rather than dense brush. Ask the rangers directly at the BIC — they know which units
are walkable and which are a punishment, and they'll tell you if you ask plainly.

## Recommended shape
**Do both, they're different trips:**
1. **Kesugi Ridge** (Denali State Park) — marked trail, no permit, 3 days, and
   **the best view of Denali anywhere on foot.** The scenery trip.
2. **Denali NP backcountry** — 2–3 nights in one unit, off-trail, no views of the
   mountain guaranteed. The wilderness trip.

Kesugi first. It's the warm-up, and it reveals how the rig and the schedule handle
you being gone for three days before you do the harder one.

# Session 5 — is one trek worth bringing the gear?

Gordon: *"Sounds like I don't have to do the harder backpacking — which then begs the
question, is a single 3-day trek worth bringing the gear?"*

## The honest math
Cost: a full kit is ~3–4 cu ft and 30–40 lbs, competing directly with the spares and
everything else in a rig with genuinely tight storage. For 3 days out of 78, that's a
bad ratio.

**But the gear isn't for one planned trek. It's for optionality.**

This is a 78-day trip built deliberately around float days and "vibes decide." Places
where a spontaneous 1–2 nighter could appear:

Kesugi Ridge (the committed one) · Kluane — King's Throne, Cottonwood · Chilkoot
out-and-back from Skagway · Tombstone/Grizzly Lake if the Dempster happens ·
Chugach State Park · Denali NP backcountry if the mood strikes · Mount Rainier ·
anywhere in BC on the way down

It also doubles as **overflow and breakdown insurance** — somewhere to sleep if the rig
is in a shop in Whitehorse for three days.

And for someone who gets bored easily, a multi-day hike is the **single highest-engagement
activity on the entire itinerary.** Nothing else on the list competes.

## ★ Recommendation: bring a stripped kit, not a full one
Not the car-camping-adjacent version. A minimal 3-season solo setup — pack, quilt or
light bag, pad, single-wall tent or tarp, canister stove, filter — compresses to
**~2 cu ft and 20–25 lbs.** At that size the optionality is obviously worth it.

**Framing that settles it: the rig is already your camping gear.** The backpacking kit
isn't buying comfort, it's buying *access* to places the rig can't reach. Priced as
access, 2 cu ft is cheap.

## Plan
- **Kesugi Ridge is the one committed trek.** Marked trail, no permit, 3 days, best
  Denali view on foot.
- Everything else is opportunistic.
- **Skip the Denali NP backcountry permit process** unless the vibe genuinely strikes —
  it's in-person only, off-trail, unbridged river crossings, and the Mile 43 closure
  limits the good units. No loss.
- **Alternative if the kit truly won't fit:** rent in Anchorage or Fairbanks. Real
  option, but it means being in a city on the right day and gambling on rental fit and
  quality for a solo 3-day trip. Prefer bringing your own.

# Session 6 — pack size correction
The Osprey is **not ~40L.** Gordon thinks 60s–80s.

Standard internal-frame sizes:
| Class | Volume | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Weekend | 30–50 L | 1–3 nights, ultralight |
| **Multiday** | **50–80 L** | **3–5+ nights** |
| Extended / expedition | 70–100+ L | A week+, winter, heavy loads |

Osprey internal frames in that range: Aether 65/70, Atmos AG 65, Aether Plus 70/85,
Volt 65/75, Rook 65, Kestrel 68. **Model and size are printed on a tag** inside the
main compartment or under the lid.

Volume when full: **65 L ≈ 2.3 cu ft · 85 L ≈ 3.0 cu ft.** For a 3-day solo trip it
won't be full — cinch it down. Still one soft object; passenger seat or footwell.
(Previous "~1.4 cu ft" figure was based on 40 L and is wrong.)

# Session 8 — Kesugi Ridge, sized honestly

Gordon: good for 2+ hrs of pickleball, **not in prime backpacking shape.**

## What the full traverse actually is
*(Commonly cited figures — confirm with Denali State Park before committing.)*
- **~27–29 mi**, Little Coal Creek → Byers Lake, usually **3–4 days**
- **Roughly 5,000–6,000 ft of total climbing** — lots of up and down along the ridge
- Opens with a sustained ~1,500–2,000 ft climb from Little Coal Creek to the ridge
- **Above treeline almost the whole way:** alpine tundra, rock, boulder fields
- **Trail is faint in places, marked by cairns** — hard to follow in fog
- Exposed to wind, rain, and fog; water on the ridge comes from tarns
- Ends with a long descent to Byers Lake
- Mid-point exit: **Ermine Hill Trail** down to the Parks Hwy splits it roughly in half

## The fitness gap
Pickleball gives good cardio and agility. What it doesn't train: **sustained climbing
with 30–35 lbs on your back, and ankles on rock and tundra hour after hour.** Doing the
full traverse at ~9 mi/day is a real effort for someone not in backpacking shape.

## Three versions
| Version | Distance | Time | Logistics |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★ **Sampler — out-and-back from Little Coal Creek** | ~8–12 mi, ~2,000 ft | 1 night | None. Climb to the ridge, camp at a tarn with the Denali view, wander, walk out. |
| Half — Little Coal Creek → Ermine Hill | ~13–15 mi | 2 days, 1 night | One shuttle/hitch between trailheads |
| Full — Little Coal Creek → Byers Lake | ~27–29 mi | 3–4 days | Shuttle to the start, rig waits at Byers Lake |

**Recommendation: plan the Sampler, decide on the ridge.** The big Denali view comes
early, right after the first climb. If you feel strong, day 2 extends along the ridge
and you still walk out the way you came. This is the cheapest way to find out whether
you want more.

Rig for the Sampler: one night at Little Coal Creek, a well-used Parks Hwy trailhead.
Lower risk than three nights, but ask the ranger at Byers Lake what they recommend.

## Training (8 months out)
- Front Range day hikes with a loaded pack, building to **~10 mi / 2,500 ft with ~30 lbs**
- Add hikes to the Nov–Apr lower-48 loop
- Stairs or a stepmill with the pack on when you can't get outside
Do the ~10 mi / 2,500 ft loaded hike comfortably by May and the Half version becomes realistic.
