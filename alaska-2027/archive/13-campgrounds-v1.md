# Campgrounds & Overnights

Names are from general knowledge — **verify status, hookups, and current pricing when
booking.** Price tiers: $ under ~$25 · $$ ~$25–45 · $$$ over ~$45.
Hookups: E = electric, W = water, S = sewer, FHU = all three, — = none.

---

## ★ Heat, refined — the nights are almost never the problem
Your rule is **overnight lows over 60°F.** Run the route against normals and, with Boise
avoided, **essentially no night on this trip crosses 60.** The heat is in the *afternoons*.

| Night | Typical low | Typical high | What you need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fairbanks, Jul (D+29–32) | ~52–55 | 72, spikes to 85+ | Friends' house; afternoon AC or shade in a hot spell |
| **Hope, Aug 14** | ~55 | ~80–85 (Fraser Canyon / Cache Creek hotter en route) | **Hookups for afternoon AC** |
| Ellensburg, Aug 17–20 | ~55 | ~86 | Family — **ask Jeff & Sara about an outlet** (below) |
| **Baker City, Aug 21** | ~48–50 | ~85 | Hookups nice for the afternoon; night is cool |
| **Arco / Craters, Aug 22** | ~48 | ~85 | Same — high desert cools fast after sunset |
| Loveland, Aug 23 | ~57 | ~87 | Home |

*Boise (low ~63) was the one real failure. The Baker City → Arco routing avoids it.*

So "cushy campgrounds for the hottest sections" means **electric hookups where you'll
be sitting in the rig on a hot afternoon:** Hope, Baker City, Arco. Arrive early, plug in,
run the AC until the sun drops.

**Ellensburg:** a roof AC can usually run from a standard 15/20A household outlet if
nothing else big is on that circuit (a soft-start helps a lot). Ask whether there's an
outlet near where you'll park, and bring a heavy-gauge 30A→15A adapter and a proper
extension cord.
