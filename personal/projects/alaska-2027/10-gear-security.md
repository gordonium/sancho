# Gear Security

Kyle's report from his mountain goat hunt checks out. Grounding:
- **Anchorage property crime runs ~49% above the national average.**
- Anchorage-area **trailhead break-ins are a documented cluster problem** — over one
  six-month stretch, Prospect Heights logged at least 7 thefts, Kincaid at least 6,
  Thunderbird Falls 4. Rangers note most go **unreported** — they find out by
  discovering broken glass in the lot.
- Trailhead lots have no consistent monitoring, and the popular ones with heavy tree
  cover are the worst.

## ⚠ Your specific exposure

### 1. ★ The CH751 problem
Most RV exterior compartment and baggage locks of this era use the **CH751 universal
key** — buyable for a few dollars by anyone. If your compartments are original, assume
**every baggage door on the rig is effectively unlocked.**
- [ ] Check whether the locks are stamped CH751
- [ ] Replace with keyed-different locks, or add hidden internal latches
This is the cheapest, highest-return security fix available.

### 2. Starlink is the single highest-value portable item
Expensive, light, universally recognizable, and it sits outside advertising itself.
**Bring it in every single time you leave, and don't leave it deployed overnight in
town.** This is a discipline problem, not a hardware problem.

### 3. 1990 locks on a 1990 truck
Cab door locks of that vintage are trivially defeated, and a 5VZ-swapped Toyota dually
is a desirable vehicle to the right thief.
- [ ] Steering wheel lock (visible deterrent — the point is that they pick a different rig)
- [ ] **Hidden fuel-pump relay interrupt** — a $15 toggle in a place only you know.
      Cheap, effective, and defeats the drive-away entirely.
- [ ] Catalytic converter: if the 3" system retains a cat, etch it or shield it

### 4. ⚠ The backpacking trailhead is the highest-risk scenario on the whole trip
A rig left unattended for 3 days at a remote trailhead is the worst case: high value,
zero witnesses, and a guaranteed multi-day window.

**Solution — don't do it.** Park in town where there's activity, and get to the
trailhead another way:
| Trip | Where to leave the rig |
|---|---|
| **Denali backcountry** | Riley Creek / visitor center area — patrolled, and the park shuttle is the access method anyway |
| **Kesugi Ridge** | Byers Lake (Denali State Park campground, staffed) rather than the Little Coal Creek pullout |
| Chilkoot | Skagway paid lots — ask at the Trail Center for the current recommendation |
| Kluane | Haines Junction, and register your trip with Parks Canada |

Paying for attended parking and hitching or shuttling to the trailhead is the correct
trade every time.

## Risk by location — where to be careful and where to relax

| Setting | Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchorage** | ⚠⚠ Highest | Don't overnight in big-box lots. Use a real RV park. |
| **Anchorage-area trailheads** | ⚠⚠ Highest | Documented clusters. Avoid unattended parking entirely. |
| Fairbanks, Whitehorse | ⚠ Moderate | Ordinary city precautions |
| Seward, Homer, Skagway | ⚠ Moderate | Tourist towns — opportunistic, not organized |
| **Yukon Gov't campgrounds** | ✓ Low | Small, remote, self-policing traveler culture |
| **BC Provincial Parks** | ✓ Low | |
| **Alaska Hwy / Cassiar corridor** | ✓ Low | You'll see the same rigs repeatedly; it's a community |
| Denali, Wrangell–St. Elias campgrounds | ✓ Low | NPS presence |

The good news: **the overwhelming majority of this itinerary is low-risk.** The
exposure is concentrated in Anchorage, the Anchorage trailheads, and unattended
backpacking parking. Plan hard around those three and relax everywhere else.

## Practical habits
- Nothing left outside overnight — including chairs, grill, and the awning out
- Nothing valuable visible through a window, ever
- Rig looks occupied when you're away: a light on a timer, no mail-slot appearance
- **Photograph and serialize everything** before departure; store the list off-rig
- ⚠ **Verify RV insurance personal-property limits.** RV policies commonly cap contents
  far below what you'll be carrying — camera gear, Starlink, backpacking kit, tools,
  and the spares add up fast. Ask the specific dollar figure and whether it covers
  theft from an unattended vehicle.
- Trackers are honest-but-limited: an AirTag needs nearby iPhones, an LTE tracker needs
  cell service. **Both work in towns and fail in the bush.** Worth having, don't rely on it.

## Kyle's context vs. yours
Hunting theft is a different problem — gear and meat caches at airstrips and spike
camps, left for days by definition, often with known access patterns. You're not
exposed to that. Your version is urban opportunism plus the trailhead window.
