## ⚠ Storage reality check

Gordon: *"I don't think any of the storage bays can hold a 27 gal tote."*
**Almost certainly right, and I over-simplified.** Research note: authoritative
Sunrader specs barely exist — even the published reviews say height, width, weight,
and tank capacities were never properly documented. Bay dimensions are not findable
online. **Measure yours.**

### Revised approach: distribute, don't consolidate
Pack in **6-qt and 12-qt shoebox totes** and soft bags that fit odd voids, not one
big container.

| Sub-kit | Volume | Weight | Container | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ★ 5VZ electrical | 0.5 cu ft | 10 lbs | 12-qt clear tote | Anywhere dry — this is the critical box |
| Cooling (hoses, belt, thermostat, tape) | 0.6 cu ft | 12 lbs | Soft bag — hoses are awkward, not rigid | Any soft void |
| Alternator | 0.4 cu ft | 12 lbs | Boxed or wrapped | **Low and centered** |
| Fluids | 0.4 cu ft | 20 lbs | Jug + milk crate | **Low, exterior bay if possible** |
| Tools | 0.8 cu ft | 30 lbs | 16" tool bag | Low |
| Tire / jack / compressor | 1.0 cu ft | 35 lbs | Loose | **Low — jack is the heaviest single item** |
| **Total** | **~3.7 cu ft** | **~120 lbs** | | |

### ★ The answer nobody mentioned: you're solo, so the cabover is free
A 21' Sunrader's cabover bunk is roughly 6' × 6' × 3' of volume. Traveling alone,
that's **dedicated storage** — vastly more than any bay, cargo box, or hitch rack,
with zero length penalty, zero aerodynamic penalty, and it's inside and dry.

Weight up there is high but it's over the front axle rather than behind the rear,
which is the better of the two bad places. Keep the heavy items (alternator, fluids,
jack) low in the bays and put bulky-light gear — **including the backpacking kit** —
in the cabover.

Known Sunrader exterior storage, per owner listings: a rear compartment (large enough
that owners slide bicycles in) and a battery compartment. Confirm against your rig.

# Session 5 — fuel tank: no free upgrade

**The Seabreeze tank is also in the teens.** So the cheap path is gone, and the
Sunrader's **16.5 usable gallons** stands (confirmed by running it dry).

## Remaining options
| Option | Gain | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **2× 5-gal jerry cans** | +10 gal ≈ **+140 mi** | ✓ **Do this.** ~$60, fully covers the 140-mi Cassiar gap. |
| Custom fabricated aluminum frame tank | +8–10 gal | Feasible — Doug can fabricate — but see scope note |
| Auxiliary tank + transfer pump (Transfer Flow) | +15–20 gal | The proper motorhome answer. Price it. |

## ⚠ Scope warning
With the swap window starting mid-April and a June departure, **a fuel tank project is
scope creep on an already-tight schedule.** The cans solve the actual blocking problem
for $60.

**Only do the tank if it can happen inside the same shop visit as the swap.** Otherwise
run the cans and revisit it after the trip.

### The quality-of-life case, for honesty
16.5 gal at 14 mpg means fueling roughly every 150 mi — about **65 fuel stops over
10,000 miles.** At 26 gal it'd be ~40. Beyond convenience, a bigger tank lets you buy
cheap fuel in Whitehorse or Fort Nelson instead of topping off at Destruction Bay
prices, which is real money over the trip. Worth pricing. Not worth risking June 7.
