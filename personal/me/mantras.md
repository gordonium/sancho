# Mantras · seed (captured 2026-09-30)

**What Gordon wants:** to be reminded of his mantras often; mixed up rather than the same one every time; and ideally relevant to what he's working on right now. v2 failed on exactly this: it picked the same one repeatedly and had no awareness of context, because "rotate, don't repeat the last one" was a sentence with no state behind it.

**Sources to gather at scaffold:**
- The desktop backgrounds Gordon made for the Rainbow Rig a few years ago (images; in cold storage on the Rig; transcribe the text off them when the Rig is next reachable, or from any copy in Sync).
- v2 `personal/mantras.md` and `personal/principles.md` (survey §9a), through the Phase 3 gate like any v2 material.
- New ones from Gordon's head: a capture session, one at a time, with the context each one belongs to.

**Design (goes to `personal/me/mantras.md` at scaffold; the file is data, the selection is a small skill step):**
- One entry per mantra, frontmatter-style: `text`, `tags` (areas, projects, moods, situations: e.g. `food`, `building`, `overcommitting`, `driving`, `client-conflict`), `source` (where it came from, with date), `added`.
- **Selection has state.** `_queue/mantras-shown.md` records which mantra was shown when. Pick by: (1) tag match to today's focus items, the open conversation's topic, or a blind spot just called (§18.3); (2) among matches, the one least recently shown; (3) never the same one within 14 days unless Gordon asks for it; (4) if nothing matches, the least recently shown overall. The state file is what v2 lacked.
- **Where it appears:** one line at the end of the morning routine (not the greeting, which stays tight); at a conversation close when the topic matched a tag; and on request ("mantra"). Never mid-task.
- **Care and feeding:** the quarterly review shows which mantras were shown, which were skipped, and which have never matched anything (a sign the tags are wrong or the mantra has aged out); Gordon adds and retires them himself.
- Tie to the watch list: a blind-spot call can carry its mantra ("you're doing the thing again; *finish before you start*"), which makes the reminder land as his own words rather than Sancho's.
