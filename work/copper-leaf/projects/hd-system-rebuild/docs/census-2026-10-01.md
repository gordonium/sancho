# Data census, part 1 (2026-10-01)

## The four findings that matter

*Read with part 2 (`census-part2-2026-10-01.md`), which settles what this part could only infer. Corrections are marked "Superseded" or "Corrected" where they apply.*

1. **The legacy data is already in WordPress.** 10,212 appointments dated 1983 to 2026, alongside 10,211 invoice records, 9,033 properties and 8,697 client records. (Corrected: draft said "each with an invoice record, a property and a client"; the links were not read in part 1. Part 2 read them: 10,203 appointments have a client link and 10,128 a property link.) The plan assumed the old system held roughly five years. It holds forty-three. Gordon adds that more legacy data sits outside WordPress, "fragmented and fractured", and needs to be audited and merged in. [gordon 2026-10-01]
2. **The reports are not.** Only 774 report posts exist, all created since 2020-11-24. The 9,442 jobs that came from v2 have a row and no report. Part 2 confirmed that no report text hides elsewhere in this database (appointments, invoices and properties have no body text). The older reports are in v2, the system Grayson built, still live; before that v1 "legacy", an Access database and Word files. [gordon 2026-10-01]
3. **2008 is a hole.** 12 appointments, against 388 the year before and 263 the year after; 89 of 2009's rows have no type. **Superseded:** the draft called this "very likely the database that got blown apart". It is not. Gordon has since described that event: a v2 bug around 2015 that deleted note selections from reports. [gordon 2026-10-01] The 2008 gap is a different, older loss; part 2 dates it (December 2007 to September 2008) and shows it was already in the data loaded into v2 in December 2014.
4. **A client is often two people.** 6,182 of 8,697 client records (71 percent) have " and " or " & " as a separate word in the name. **Narrowed by part 2:** a second person is named on about 96 percent of client rows up to 2009 and on 14 to 22 percent since. The plan's `first_name, last_name` columns need a second pair, as v2 had; not a household structure.

## How this was read

Read-only `GET` requests from the logged-in admin page of the dev clone to the site's own REST API, counting with the `X-WP-Total` header, plus title-only listings analysed inside the browser so that only totals came back. No client's details were copied out; nothing was saved, edited or clicked. One appointment's heading (a name and an address) was seen while probing a page layout and is recorded nowhere. The site runs an activity-log plugin, which may have logged these reads as Gordon's session; that is the site's doing, not a change made here.

Site: WordPress 7.1.2, 39 plugins (38 active), theme "Copper Leaf Creative Master Theme" 2.15.2 on Genesis. Logged-in user id 13.

## Duplicates (by title only)

Titles are what the API exposes. Property titles are the address; contact titles are the name.

**Properties (9,030).** 8,947 start with a house number. 8,384 have exactly one comma ("number street, town"); 634 have two (a state or unit as well); the state is absent from most titles.
- Identical titles: 10 pairs.
- Identical after lower-casing, stripping punctuation and normalising Road/Rd and the like: 29 pairs.
- Same street line and same town: 79 groups, 160 rows.
So the property table is already close to unique by address text: 81 surplus rows (0.9 percent), 160 rows involved. That is a lower bound: most titles lack the state, and text matching cannot find a misspelt street. Part 2 confirms the migration deduplicated properties (952 were merged into an existing one).

**Clients (8,697).** 155 names occur more than once (314 rows; the largest group is 3). 2,388 are a plain two-word name; 201 contain a slash (these categories overlap with the "and" count). Same name is not the same person, so this is an upper bound on easy merges, not a count of duplicates. Email is the real key and could not be read (below).

**Brokers (998).** 41 names occur more than once (84 rows).

## What could not be read through the browser, and what will

Field values (ACF fields stored as post meta) are not exposed by the REST API, because the field groups are registered with `show_in_rest` off [doc:survey-hdonline.md line 348]. WordPress's export returns zero items for these post types. The plugin's own View Appointment page shows the fields but is 866 KB and takes 8 seconds per appointment (the notes selector is printed on every admin page [doc:survey-hdonline.md §9 item 15]), which makes even a sample costly on a metered connection. So these remain open:

- which client, property and invoice fields are actually filled, by era (the question plan §10.1 says "decides everything below");
- how many clients share an email address (the dedup key in plan §3);
- invoice totals and paid flags;
- the exact count of compiled reports;
- how appointments link to properties and clients in the legacy rows (the serialized-array relations [doc:survey-hdonline.md §9 item 41]).

## What this changes in the plan

1. **Phase 2 is mostly a data migration and an audit, not a PDF-printing job from WordPress.** Of the 774 reports in WordPress, the 125 from the last year go to Google Docs (R7.1); the other 649 need a PDF: 106 compiled inspection reports and about 540 older consult letters. The 9,442 jobs that came from v2 have no report in WordPress; their reports must be rendered from v2. Plan step 4 also says "Same for invoices": there are 10,211 invoice records, and whether Gordon wants the legacy ones printed is open.
2. **R7.8: the job and contact rows exist back to 1983, in one place, and part 2 shows they are substantial** (fee, address and property facts on nearly every row). Coverage is thin before 1996 (287 jobs in thirteen years) and empty for most of 2008. They can be read from v2's own tables, which are in this database. What is not here is the report content and whatever the other sources hold. [gordon 2026-10-01]
3. **The old reports are the missing piece.** Gordon has now said what the sources are (v2 by Grayson, v1 "legacy", an Access database, Word files) [gordon 2026-10-01]; where the backups and files are is the next question. Until they are read, "an appointment record for every historical job with its PDF attached" (R7.6) can be met for the record and not for the PDF.
4. **The 2008 hole is now a number, and it is a second loss, not the one in R7.4.** By month (part 2): one job in December 2007, none from January to September 2008, then a slow restart. At the surrounding rate that is roughly 200 to 350 missing jobs. (Corrected: the draft said 400 to 500, which exceeded both neighbouring years.) An estimate, not a count.
5. **The data model needs room for a second person on a client**, as v2 had. Dedup on email works for clients from about 2010 on (94 percent have one); before that, on name plus phone plus address (2 percent of 1990s clients have an email).
6. **Properties: carry the existing deduplication forward** and add the geocoded key. At least 80 groups look alike by address text; geocoding 9,033 addresses, most without a state in the title, will find more. Size unknown until it is tried.
7. **Design load: 100 to 130 jobs a year, about 10,000 historical jobs, about 40,000 records across the five types.** SQLite is comfortable at that size; nothing here argues for a database server.
8. **Brokers and attorneys are in the data** (998 and 98, some of them also clients). The plan drops them as roles going forward [doc:plan.md §1]; R7.8 means their records still need a home. One contacts table with a role per link fits better than a separate legacy table. Part 2: broker links exist on 732 v3-era appointments, so they are not only legacy.
